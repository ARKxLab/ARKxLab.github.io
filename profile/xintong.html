About

Short Bio

Dr. Xin Tong is an Assistant Professor in the Computational Media and Arts Thrust at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou). She was a postdoctoral scholar supported by the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada (NSERC) at Stanford University. She obtained both her Master’s and PhD degrees from Simon Fraser University in Canada, where her doctoral dissertation received the 2021 Canadian Human-Computer Communications Society (CHCCS) Best Dissertation Award.

Her research focuses on intelligent HCI and human-AI collaboration, human–robot collaborative co-creation processes and interaction mechanisms. She has published more than 60 papers in top-tier international HCI conferences and journals and won several Best Paper Awards at these conferences. She has received grants from NSFC, the National Foreign Talent Program, CCF–Lenovo Blue Ocean Research Grant, the Guangzhou City–University Joint Grant, etc. She was also awarded the 2025 CCF HCI Committee Young Scholar Award and the Outstanding Individual Award from the Kunshan Science and Technology Association.

She has also been one of the organizing committee members in CHI 2026, DIS 2026, IDC 2025, CHI Play 2025 and 2024, HHME 2026 and 2023, ChineseCHI 2022, etc., and reviewers for top-tier conferences.

Research

Human-Centered HCI, AI and Creative Media

I work on real-world human-centered challenges at the intersection of human-computer interaction, artificial intelligence, and creative media. My research focuses on understanding how people with physical, cognitive, and psychological disabilities experience technology, and on designing, developing, and evaluating interactive systems that better support their needs.

AI + Interactive Technologies for Accessibility and Health

Serious games, tangible interfaces, and educational tools that support people with autism, dementia, and other special needs in real-world learning, healthcare, and everyday contexts.

VR/AR + Generative AI for Education and Digital Cultural Heritage

Immersive history learning, AI-generated mural restoration, empathy-building experiences, and embodied interactions with cultural heritage.

Human-AI Collaboration for Creativity and Problem-Solving

Robot-assisted painting, human-AI interface co-generation, AI-supported avatar design, and generative AI for competitive programming.

Join Us

Prospective Ph.D. and MPhil Applicants

Please read this message carefully before you send any inquiry email to me. MPhil+PhD offer many advantages over direct PhD. Our Red Bird MPhil program trains interdisciplinary research and teamwork abilities that are essential for addressing complex multi-disciplinary challenges humans are facing in today's world.

With two years of MPhil training, you will gain a much clearer vision of whether and how you want to pursue a PhD. Consequently, you will be much better prepared to get your PhD done in as short as two years (though shorter does not necessarily mean better — many Chinese students are too obsessed with completing degrees faster).

MPhil+PhD provides you with sufficient time to think through how you may turn your innovative MPhil/PhD research into real-world impactful prototypes. Thus, whether you want to pursue a PhD or not, I strongly encourage you to apply to get into our Red Bird MPhil program first. Many of my MPhil students continue to do PhD with me or in other top-tier places. A few of my MPhil graduates even started their own startups. Follow the instructions via this link to apply for MPhil.

Below are three areas in which I look for MPhil+PhD students:

  1. AI + Interactive Technologies (e.g., serious games, tangible interfaces, educational tools) for Accessibility and Health Challenges (e.g., supporting people with autism, dementia, and other special needs in real-world learning, healthcare, and everyday contexts)
  2. VR/AR + Generative AI for Education and Digital Cultural Heritage (e.g., immersive history learning, AI-generated mural restoration, empathy-building experiences, and embodied interactions with cultural heritage)
  3. Human-AI Collaboration for Creativity and Problem-Solving (e.g., robot-assisted painting, human-AI interface co-generation, AI-supported avatar design, and generative AI for competitive programming)
