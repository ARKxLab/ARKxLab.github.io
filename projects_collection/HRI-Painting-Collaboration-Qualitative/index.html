From Tool to Partner: Expressive Behaviors as the Bridge to Human-Robot Creative Collaboration

Jie Yu, Xinyi Zhang, Yaqiong Luo, RAY LC, Kaishun Wu, Xin Tong

Abstract

Expressive robotic behaviors foster the transition from command-response interactions to authentic creative partnerships. By contrasting functional-only and expressive conditions in human-robot figure drawing tasks, this study demonstrates how acknowledgment gestures and spatial coordination cultivate emotional connection, engagement, and collaborative satisfaction.

Key Highlights

  • Identifies expressive behavior patterns that signal partnership intent, including inviting gestures and acknowledgment cues.
  • Reveals measurable shifts in perceived collaboration quality between functional-only and expressive robot conditions.
  • Provides actionable design guidelines for embodying expressive behaviors in future co-creative robotic systems.

Study Design

We conducted a formative probe with five artist participants to surface perceptual cues that convey expressivity in a robot arm. These insights informed the implementation of expressive behaviors, contrasted against a purely functional baseline.

Eighteen participants then completed within-subject figure drawing sessions with both robot behaviors. Quantitative measures and interview feedback captured collaboration quality, perceived partnership, and creative satisfaction.

The expressive condition consistently evoked higher engagement, stronger emotional bonding, and richer sense of co-presence, evidencing the transformative role of expressive robotics in creative collaboration contexts.
